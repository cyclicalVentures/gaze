# Gaze recorder

Mode: Operate. The second Gaze tab lets a user calibrate a webcam, record where they look and review the session against an optional game recording. Keep the existing Space Grotesk, dark violet, teal and coral visual system. The desktop working surface places the live map or playback first, with a compact setup and history sidebar. On phones, setup and history follow the map in the same page. Fine rules organize sections; the map, tracking status and current action carry the hierarchy.

The header exposes 3D viewer and Gaze recorder as keyboard-operable tabs. The recorder can open at `#recorder`. Tab switching is locked during calibration, recording preparation, capture and saving; returning to another tool stops the recorder camera and screen sharing. An idle recorder begins with an explicit Enable camera action, webcam selector and Tracking area selector. Show camera readiness, eye visibility, a live gaze dot and the current coordinate scope. Start recording requires an applied calibration and a fresh on-screen estimate. Session naming is optional; Stop & review opens the completed recording.

Entire monitor scope is for desktop game sessions. Calibrate on the monitor used for play: the action requests native browser fullscreen and checks that the page covers the display. This page scope calibrates the visible page area and is the foreground workflow for iOS and browsers without native fullscreen support. Physical measurements distinguish entire monitor width from visible page width. Changing page dimensions, display geometry or relevant zoom settings invalidates an incompatible mapping and stops an affected session. Page coordinates must never be presented as gaze across other apps.

Calibration first holds a center fixation with a three-second countdown, then uses the existing protected-focus calibration dialog. Collect nine targets and five held-out checks at each of normal, near and far distances. Preserve live distance guidance, tracking-loss feedback, pause, cancel, retry, measured CSS-pixel errors and explicit application. Successful profiles save in local browser storage for the actual webcam device ID, under separate monitor and page scopes; neither replaces the 3D viewer's profile. Restore compatible profiles after camera activation, explain mismatches and expose Use saved profile and Forget profile. If saving fails, an applied profile can still work for the current visit.

Game footage is optional. Where display capture and MediaRecorder are supported, Attach game screen opens the browser's screen chooser before recording starts. Accept only an entire monitor with a compatible aspect ratio; prompt the user to confirm it is the calibrated monitor because matching shape does not establish monitor identity. Capture has no audio. Keep gaze-only recording available when screen capture is unsupported, declined or removed. The webcam preview supports positioning. Gaze-only capture saves coordinates, while optional screen footage records everything visible on the selected monitor, including that preview.

During recording, show elapsed time, sample count, current gaze visibility and a persistent Stop & review action. Save session metadata and gaze checkpoints to IndexedDB every five seconds, and persist screen-video chunks as they arrive. Session history shows names, dates and durations, marks unfinished checkpoints as recovered and offers an explicit Delete/Keep confirmation for deleting gaze and video. Local storage failure leaves gaze available in memory with a clear export instruction; video recording stops if it cannot be persisted. Two hours of elapsed session time or 512 MB of screen video stops the session, with an explanation and review of the available data. Browser quota or a write backlog may stop video sooner.

The recording guidance explains the operating limits beside the action. Keep the webcam fixed and use the calibrated monitor; keep Gaze visible when possible. Desktop monitor capture attempts to continue across visibility changes, but hidden or minimized browsers may throttle or suspend sampling. Page-scope capture stops when the page is backgrounded. The camera stopping, calibration changes, screen-sharing end and navigation can also end a session. Checkpoints permit recovery of what reached storage; they do not guarantee the last seconds survive a suspended or closed page.

Playback preserves the calibrated aspect ratio and overlays the cursor, optional recent gaze trail and optional dwell heatmap on recorded screen video when available. Provide play/pause, restart, scrubbing, speed and From/To range controls. The selected range drives the heatmap, coverage and most-viewed-area table. Show on-screen coverage, off-screen time, unobserved time and the calibration's average check error. Account only for short observed intervals, capped at 150 ms per sample; do not fill gaze across blinks, loss or suspension gaps. The timeline and textual totals make missing coverage visible, while the region table supplies a textual counterpart to the map.

Review exports the full session as JSON, gaze samples as CSV, the current map canvas as PNG and any screen video separately as WebM or MP4. The PNG contains the visible gaze overlay on a transparent background; screen footage is in the separate video download. If this browser cannot play a stored video, explain the failure and retain gaze-only review and downloads. A New recording action returns to capture setup without deleting history. Keep exports and local-storage limits understandable without claiming device-level accuracy or uninterrupted background recording.
